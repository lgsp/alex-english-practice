Case Study: Jeffrey Epstein at Dalton School

An analysis of behavior and institutional norms

Article Summary

In the mid-1970s, Jeffrey Epstein was hired as a math teacher at the prestigious Dalton School. Despite his background (he lacked a college degree), he was recruited for being "interesting." However, his conduct quickly raised eyebrows. He often pushed the limits of the school's strict dress code, wearing fur coats and gold chains.

Former students recall an unsettling figure who acted more like a friend than an authority. His habit of focusing on teenage girls left a lasting impression on many alumni. Ultimately, he was dismissed for poor performance, as he did not come up to snuff as a professional educator.
