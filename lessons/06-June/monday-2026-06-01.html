Key Video Expressions

Essential conversational tools and high-leverage communication phrases used by Vinh Giang.

Expression 1

To break the ice

To say or do something that relieves tension, awkwardness, or social friction when meeting someone new.

Real-Life Example: "Instead of talking about the bad weather, he asked a unique question about my hobbies to break the ice."

Expression 2

To be in your own head

To be overthinking, highly self-conscious, or consumed by internal anxieties instead of staying present.

Real-Life Example: "I couldn't enjoy the networking event because I was too in my own head about saying the wrong thing."

Expression 3

To pick up on something

To notice, detect, or correctly interpret subtle non-verbal cues, micro-expressions, or shifts in vocal tone.

Real-Life Example: "Great communicators immediately pick up on tiny changes in body language to shift the flow of a meeting."

Expression 4

To build rapport

To establish a relationship of mutual trust, understanding, harmony, and smooth fluid communication with another individual.

Real-Life Example: "Matching the structural pacing and rate of speech of your client is a fast way to build authentic professional rapport."

Expression 5

To shift the spotlight

To direct the attention or focus of the conversation away from yourself and onto the other person.

Real-Life Example: "When they asked me an uncomfortable question about my personal life, I tried to shift the spotlight back onto their career achievements."

Expression 6

To leave someone hanging

To keep someone waiting for a response, or to leave a conversational statement incomplete, causing awkward suspense.

Real-Life Example: "Don't just say 'Guess what happened yesterday' and then leave me hanging for three hours!"

Expression 7

To carry the conversation

To do almost all the talking or put in all the social effort to keep a conversation going when the other person is passive.

Real-Life Example: "He was so shy that I had to carry the conversation for the entire dinner, which was quite exhausting."

Expression 8

To drop a hook

To intentionally include an interesting or open-ended detail in your answer to invite the other person to ask more questions.

Real-Life Example: "When answering where I live, I always drop a hook by mentioning I spent a year living in an old train car."

Expression 9

To be second nature

When a skill or behavior has been practiced so much that it becomes completely automatic, requiring no conscious thought.

Real-Life Example: "After years of teaching online, managing multiple windows and checking the code layout has become second nature to me."

Expression 10

To put someone at ease

To make someone feel relaxed, safe, comfortable, and free from anxiety or nervousness in a social setting.

Real-Life Example: "Her warm smile and slow, calm way of speaking immediately put the nervous candidate at ease during the interview."
